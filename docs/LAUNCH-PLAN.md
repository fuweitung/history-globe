### C. Nice to have, can wait until after launch

- Quiz mode for students (on the Roadmap “Later” list)

- Crowded people labels on busy modern maps; the few civilisations without a free picture

### D. Risks to keep in mind

| Risk | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Someone finds a serious mistake on day one | The beta bar and report form already set expectations; reply quickly, fix, and say thank you in public. |
| A disputed topic causes an argument | Point to the neutral wording and sources; correct only with a cited source; never delete without a reason. |
| Many reports arrive at once | Keep a simple triage list (wrong / disputed / missing source / feature) and handle sensitive topics first. |
| Only two people maintain it | Say so plainly, and welcome teachers and students as reviewers. |

## 4. Suggested order

> **Top priority now (Fuwei, set 18 Nov 2026):** the site is live and open to search engines, but item 5 is not done. Read the six disputed topics (Hong Kong 2019–20, Crimea, Cyprus, Western Sahara, Nagorno-Karabakh, Rohingya) against the new wording and the “Open (needs a person)” list in `docs/sensitive-review.md`, then agree item 6 (who reads the Report-a-mistake form) with Mike. Do this before anything else on this plan.

1. **Now (while Mike finishes #78):** approve this plan; decide items 1, 4 and 14; choose the hosting route.
2. **Preparation (about a week):** Claude does items 2, 3, 8, 11, 12 and 16 in small pull requests; owner records the video (item 9) and tests phones (item 10).
3. **Review gate:** decide item 5 (expert read or clear marking) and item 6 (who reads reports). Do not launch until both have an answer.
4. **Dry run:** put the site online privately, ask 3–5 friends or teachers to try it for a few days, fix what they find.
5. **Launch day:** switch the repository to public, remove `noindex`, publish the GitHub release, then post on X, LinkedIn and Threads in that order, a few hours apart.
6. **First week:** answer every report, fix the top mistakes, thank people publicly.
