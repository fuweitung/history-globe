# Second-source spot-check: the 20 major civilisations

Checked 8–9 Nov 2026 (issue #77) by reading **Encyclopaedia Britannica** articles, with **World History Encyclopedia** (worldhistory.org) where Britannica did not cover a point. No Wikipedia pages were used for this check.

**How Britannica was read.** This time britannica.com could be read with an ordinary automated fetch (the earlier check got HTTP 403). But most articles showed only an **excerpt**, often just the first 100 words, so many details could not be seen. Nothing was done to get round this (no print pages, mirrors or caches). Where an article page failed to load or the excerpt did not cover a point, a Britannica biography page or the World History Encyclopedia article was read instead. Points that neither source showed are listed as *not checked*.

Each fact that a source supported has that source added, with “(checked 8 Nov 2026)” or “(checked 9 Nov 2026)” for the day the page was read. Each entry’s *Overall certainty* item now sums up the result.

| Civilisation | Sources read | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Roman Empire | Britannica: Roman Empire (excerpt); WHE: Roman Empire | **Agrees.** From 27 BCE (Augustus first emperor), centred on Rome, height c. 117, western end 4 Sept 476 when Odoacer deposed Romulus Augustulus (WHE notes some prefer 480); east continued to 1453. |
| Han China | Britannica: Han dynasty (excerpt); Britannica: Wudi | **Agrees, one date convention differs.** Founder Liu Bang, capitals Chang’an then Luoyang, end 220 and the Three Kingdoms; expansion under Emperor Wu (141–87 BCE). Britannica starts the dynasty in **206 BCE** (this app: 202 BCE) and gives Wang Mang’s Xin as **9–25 CE** (this app: 9–23). Both noted. Xuchang not covered. |
| Ottoman Empire | Britannica: Ottoman Empire (excerpt); Britannica: Süleyman the Magnificent | **Agrees.** Founded by Osman I about 1300; capitals Bursa, Edirne, Istanbul; Süleyman 1520–66; ended 1922, replaced by the Turkish Republic. Britannica does not say the empire peaked under Süleyman (peak not checked). |
| Mughal Empire | Britannica: Mughal dynasty (excerpt); Britannica: Babur; Britannica: Bahadur Shah II | **Agrees.** Babur founder after Panipat (21 Apr 1526); effective rule to the mid-18th century, then a reduced power to the mid-19th; Bahadur Shah II (r. 1837–57) exiled to Burma. Capitals and peak not covered. |
| Inca Empire | Britannica: Inca (excerpt); WHE: Inca Civilization | **Mostly agrees.** Pachacuti’s defeat of the Chanca 1438, capital Cusco, Atahualpa captured 1532 and executed 1533. WHE’s timeline also gives c. 1425 (Viracocha) for the start of expansion and dates the **greatest extent to about 1530**, slightly after Huayna Capac’s reign, which this app calls the peak. Noted; Vilcabamba 1572 not covered. |
| Aztec Empire | Britannica: Aztec (full article) | **Agrees.** Tenochtitlan founded 1325; alliance with Texcoco and Tlacopan under Itzcoatl (1428–40); Moctezuma II 1502–20; Spanish control of Tenochtitlan by 13 Aug 1521. |
| Mongol Empire | Britannica: Mongol empire (excerpt) | **Agrees.** Began 1206 (Temüjin proclaimed Genghis Khan); Karakorum; about 9 million sq mi (≈23 million km²); Great Khans Ögedei, Güyük, Möngke, Kublai; split into the Golden Horde, Chagatai, Ilkhanate and Kublai’s China. The 1368 end was not in the excerpt. |
| Abbasid Caliphate | Britannica: Abbasid caliphate (excerpt) | **Agrees.** 750–1258; first caliph al-Saffah; capital moved to Baghdad; fell in the Mongol siege of 1258. Britannica places the high point more broadly (750–833: al-Mansur, Harun al-Rashid, al-Maʾmun); noted. |
| Byzantine Empire | Britannica: Byzantine Empire (excerpt); WHE: Byzantine Empire | **Agrees.** 330–1453 (Constantinople founded 11 May 330; WHE notes 476 as another proposed start); Justinian 527–565, Basil II 976–1025, Constantine XI 1449–53; ended 29 May 1453 with Mehmed II’s conquest. |
| Tang Dynasty | Britannica: Tang dynasty (excerpt); WHE: Tang Dynasty | **Agrees.** 618–907; founder Li Yuan (Gaozu); apex early 8th century, Xuanzong; An Lushan Rebellion 755–763; ended 907 after Zhu Wen had the last emperors killed. |
| Song Dynasty | Britannica: Song dynasty | **Agrees.** 960–1279; founder Zhao Kuangyin; Kaifeng then Lin’an (Hangzhou) after 1127; ended 1279 when the Song fleet was destroyed near Guangzhou (Yamen). |
| Ming China | Britannica: History of China – The Ming dynasty | **Agrees.** 1368–1644; founder Zhu Yuanzhang (Hongwu); Nanjing to 1420, move to Beijing completed 1421; Li Zicheng took Beijing in April 1644 and the Chongzhen emperor killed himself; loyalists resisted in the south. |
| Qing China | Britannica: Qing dynasty (excerpt); Britannica: Puyi | **Agrees.** 1644–1911/12, name first used 1636; Puyi abdicated 12 Feb 1912 in response to the revolution. Peak and founders not covered. |
| Maurya Empire | Britannica: Mauryan Empire (excerpt); WHE: Mauryan Empire | **Agrees.** c. 321 (WHE: 322)–185 BCE; founder Chandragupta with Chanakya; capital Pataliputra; Ashoka 268–232 BCE; Brihadratha killed by Pushyamitra Shunga in 185 BCE (WHE timeline says 187); noted. |
| Gupta Empire | Britannica: Gupta dynasty (excerpt) | **Agrees.** First emperor Chandragupta I (Licchavi marriage); expansion under Samudragupta and Chandragupta II; Huna invasions; ended by the mid-6th century. Capital not covered. |
| Achaemenid Persia | Britannica: Achaemenian dynasty (excerpt) | **Agrees.** 559–330 BCE (this app’s 550 start already noted as an alternative); founder Cyrus II; widest reach under Darius I and Xerxes I; ended with Darius III after his defeat by Alexander (330 BCE). Only Pasargadae and Persepolis named. |
| Sasanian Empire | Britannica: Sasanian dynasty (excerpt); Britannica: Yazdegerd III | **Agrees.** 224–651; Ardashir I (224–241); destroyed by the Arabs 637–651; al-Qadisiyyah 636/637, Nahavand 642; Yazdegerd III killed at Merv in 651. |
| Carthage | Britannica: Carthage (excerpt); WHE: Carthage | **Agrees.** Traditional founding from Tyre in 814 BCE; most powerful Mediterranean state before the Punic Wars; destroyed 146 BCE, land became the province of Africa. Independence from Tyre not covered. |
| Assyria | Britannica: Assyria (short article) | **Differs on the start.** This app starts with the city-state of Ashur (c. 2025 BCE); Britannica says Assyria was dependent on Babylonia and Mitanni for most of the 2nd millennium and became independent in the 14th century BCE. End agrees (Chaldean–Median coalition, 612–609 BCE). Britannica puts the height under Tiglath-Pileser III, Sargon II, Sennacherib and Esarhaddon, with Ashurbanipal the last great king. Overall certainty now *disputed* with both views. |
| Melaka Sultanate | Britannica: Melaka (excerpt) | **Agrees.** Founded about 1400 by Paramesvara of Temasek (Singapore); Malay rule ended 1511 when Albuquerque took it for Portugal. Peak and conversion to Islam not covered. |

## Corrections

No wrong facts were found. All differences come from different conventions (Han 206 or 202 BCE, Xin dynasty to 23 or 25 CE, Assyria’s start, Maurya 185 or 187 BCE, the Inca peak). They are now recorded in each entry’s *Overall certainty*, and Assyria’s is now marked *disputed*.
